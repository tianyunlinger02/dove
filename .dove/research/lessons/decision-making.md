# Decision making

Researcher-owned, optional guidance; applicability and evidence matter more than repeated use. See [Lessons](LESSONS.md) for ownership and advice maturity.

## Choose work by real value

Prioritize truth, safety, and evidence integrity; real task results and long-term value; scientific value and sound architecture; simplicity and maintainability; then execution efficiency and presentation. Navigation, record keeping, demonstrations, and local metrics help only when they improve a decision or substantive result.

Distinguish necessary work, diagnostic work, trustworthy infrastructure, final evidence, and unnecessary work. A smaller diagnostic can test a premise, but cannot silently remove a necessary final capability or redefine the goal around what already runs. Assess problem value, method specificity, argument maturity, novelty, evidence, and cost separately rather than with one progress score.

Use suitable existing code, data, models, tools, compute, and prior results when task fit, quality, license, fairness, reproducibility, and safety permit. Resources accelerate the chosen question; they do not choose it. Do not default to the weakest hardware or build unused compatibility paths, and do not inherit the broad question's value after shrinking it into an insignificant implementation patch.

## Match investment to the unresolved premise

Decompose the goal into candidates and actionable units while preserving overall mechanism, value, and dependencies. Complete low-cost reversible in-scope choices directly when they do not change the core research judgment. Before expanding cost, dependencies, or claim strength, inspect the premise most likely to invalidate substantial work; absorb a useful segment's feedback before scaling, not after every small operation.

Choose the action most likely to change the decision. Reuse checked evidence while its conditions hold. Repeat investigation for new evidence, changed conditions, contradictions, or a new decision question; when more inspection would not alter the next action, proceed while retaining nonblocking unknowns.

Distinguish core issues affecting the central goal, branch issues affecting dependent work, and local quality issues. Severity follows consequences and dependencies, not repair effort; absent support is not refutation. Carry important delegated and reviewer objections into the next investment decision, or explain non-adoption using checked evidence. An unresolved core objection cannot disappear into an old report.

After a meaningful result, continue the strongest supported route, switch when another becomes better, or stop when feasible work cannot resolve the important uncertainty. Repair shared causes and preserve valid work and informative negative results. Past effort does not oblige a prototype, patch, or failed route to enter the final method. Recognizing refutation needs no approval; changing the user's research goal does, and mention of another direction is not authorization to switch.

Judge progress by representative inputs reaching useful results and by changed evidence or decisions, not by analysis, tests, or documents produced. A bounded support task can finish without becoming mainline completion.
